Find Your Center

Before deciding how your brand should look, find the beliefs, emotions, and strengths underneath it.

Your name, business name, project name, or a working name is perfect.

There is no wrong choice.

Why this matters: The strongest brands usually feel emotionally consistent. People may forget your exact words, but they remember whether your work made them feel capable, understood, energized, safe, curious, bold, or hopeful.

Choose 2–3 feelings. These will become a filter for your words, colors, images, and content.

Think advice, skills, perspective, energy, problem-solving, taste, or support.

Recall moments when you were not performing or trying to impress anyone.

This does not need to be controversial. A belief can simply be a standard you care deeply about.

Example: “I believe people learn faster when they feel respected, not talked down to.” That belief can shape a brand’s voice, teaching style, visuals, and customer experience.
If this feels emotional, that is okay. Personal does not mean public. You can let an experience shape your values without sharing every detail of the story. Your brand should have boundaries.
