Put Your Personal Brand Into Practice

You have already defined the important parts of your personal brand. Now organize the visual choices that support it in the tool that feels easiest.

This is an implementation step—not the outcome. Your personal brand is how you want to show up, communicate, and be remembered. Canva, Google Slides, or Microsoft simply give you a place to apply the visual part consistently.
What is Canva? Canva is an easy, browser-based design tool for making presentations, social posts, documents, mood boards, and reusable templates. It is popular because you can drag and drop instead of learning professional design software. There is a free version.
Use the tool you already know

Canva is our easiest recommendation, but it is not required. The goal is to give your visual choices one organized home.

Canva
Best for easy drag-and-drop design, mood boards, social graphics, templates, and a visual brand home.

You are not deciding who you are here. You already did that. This step is just organizing and applying the visual choices that support your personal brand.
1

Create one home for your visual choices

Canva: create a Brand Kit if your plan includes it, or make a folder and multi-page design called My Brand Home. Google Slides or PowerPoint: create one presentation called My Brand Home.

2

Add the choices you already made

Bring in your colors, headline font, body font, image direction, visual references, logo versions if you have them, and the feeling you want your presence to create.

3

Create a few useful templates

Only make what you will actually use: perhaps one social post, one presentation/page, one quote or teaching graphic, and one promotional layout. Two or three is enough to start.

4

Save a mini visual rules page

Include your brand feelings, colors and their jobs, font roles, image style, logo rules if you have a logo, and a few do / don’t examples.

The “remove the logo” test
Imagine the logo disappears. Does the design still support the impression you want to create because of the color, typography, imagery, spacing, and voice? If yes, your visual choices are doing their job.
