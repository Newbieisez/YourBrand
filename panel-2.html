Who You’re Here For

A brand gets clearer when you know whose life or work you want to make better.

Describe a real person, not “everyone.” What season are they in? What are they trying to do?

What feels frustrating, lonely, confusing, expensive, time-consuming, or important to them?

Think about the practical change, outcome, or progress you hope to help create.

Do not force uniqueness. Look for a meaningful combination of how you think, work, teach, care, simplify, challenge, or create.

Good differentiation is often a combination, not a gimmick. “Strategic + warm,” “creative + rigorous,” “luxury + approachable,” “direct + deeply empathetic” can be more believable than trying to invent an entirely new category.
