5

Build It in Canva

Now turn the thinking into a visual home you can actually use every week.

Before you open Canva: have your 2–3 brand feelings, rough color direction, font direction, logo if you have one, and a small mood board. Canva should help you organize and create—not decide who you are.
You do not have to “be a designer.” Start simple. Canva gives you a place to collect your choices and practice using them. Your first version can change. The goal is not perfection—it is creating enough consistency that your brand starts to feel recognizable.
1

Create one home for your brand

If you use Canva Pro or Business, go to Brand and create a Brand Kit. If you use Canva Free, create a folder called My Brand and a simple multi-page design called My Brand Home.

2

Add your logo versions

Keep a primary logo, a simple version, an icon/mark if you have one, and light/dark versions. Avoid stretching, recoloring randomly, or putting the logo on backgrounds where it disappears.

Important: If you plan to trademark a logo, do not rely on a stock Canva logo template or stock graphic as the final logo. Use original artwork or work with a designer for the final mark.
3

Save your colors with their exact codes

Use HEX codes for digital work. Label what each color is for: main, background, text, accent, highlight. This prevents “close enough” colors from creeping in.

4

Choose your fonts and give them jobs

Do not collect six “favorite” fonts. Decide which one is for headlines and which one is for readable body copy.

5

Add image examples, not just rules

Save 6–12 photos or visual examples that show the lighting, people, composition, texture, and energy you want. Add a few examples of what you do NOT want too.

6

Create 5 starter templates

Start with what you will actually use: one social post, one story/reel cover, one presentation/page, one quote or teaching graphic, and one simple promotional or lead-magnet layout.

7

Save a mini brand rules page

Include: your brand feelings, color use, font jobs, image style, logo rules, voice reminders, and 3 “do / don’t” examples.

The “remove the logo” test
Open one of your designs and imagine the logo is gone. Does it still feel like your brand because of the color, typography, images, spacing, and voice? If yes, you are building an identity. If not, the logo is doing too much work.
